# Deliverability Center

> Writing a newsletter and wondering why email is not arriving? Read the [deliverability guide](deliverability-guide.md): how to read the check-up, every finding and its fix, and a troubleshooting flow. This page describes how the feature is built.

Settings → Sending → Deliverability (`/channels/<id>/deliverability`) shows, for one Channel, how mailbox providers treat its mail: DMARC alignment per sending domain, a breakdown by mailbox provider, the complaint rate against Gmail's thresholds, Reputation Ramp history, and dedicated sending. Tables: `dmarc_reports`, `dmarc_records` and the `channels` dedicated-sending columns (`migrations/0012_platform.sql`, `0018_deliverability.sql`). Shared constants: `packages/domain/src/p7-platform.ts`; request and response schemas: `p7-deliverability.ts`.

## How DMARC reports flow in

1. **The record.** The DMARC record Swarm recommends for a sending domain is

   ```
   _dmarc.<domain>  TXT  v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; adkim=r; aspf=r; rua=mailto:dmarc+<sendingDomainId>@<DMARC_MAIL_DOMAIN>
   ```

   `p=quarantine` is the least the first-send check accepts, so publishing the recommendation never blocks sending. Every domain gets its own address, so a report identifies its domain without trusting anything inside it. Domains added before Phase 7 were shown a shared address; the Sending page and the Deliverability Center now show them the per-domain record, and the "published record" check on the Deliverability page says when DNS still has the old one.

2. **Authorising the report address.** The report address is on the platform's domain, not the sending domain, so reporters check RFC 7489 §7.1 external-destination verification: a TXT record `<sending domain>._report._dmarc.<DMARC_MAIL_DOMAIN>` containing `v=DMARC1`. The platform publishes it once as a wildcard, `*._report._dmarc.<DMARC_MAIL_DOMAIN>  TXT  "v=DMARC1"` (docs/deployment.md), which authorises every sending domain. Reporters that skip the check send anyway; reporters that do the check and find nothing send nothing.

3. **Email Routing.** Mail to `dmarc+*@<DMARC_MAIL_DOMAIN>` reaches the events worker's `email()` handler. Anything else on that domain is dropped. `unsub+<code>@<UNSUBSCRIBE_MAIL_DOMAIN>` keeps its own handling.

4. **Parsing** (`packages/engine/src/dmarc-mime.ts`, `dmarc.ts`):
   - The message (at most 10 MB) is split into MIME parts (at most 5 levels and 50 parts). The report is the part typed or named as zip, gzip or XML, else any part whose first bytes are a zip, a gzip or XML; an XML body sent inline counts too.
   - zip is read with a small reader (stored and deflate entries, at most 5, no ZIP64, no encryption); gzip and deflate use `DecompressionStream`. The attachment may be at most 5 MB and the XML at most 25 MB, counted while inflating, so a zip bomb or a lying size header stops at the limit.
   - The XML reader handles elements, text, CDATA, comments, the five predefined entities and character references. A `DOCTYPE` is refused, so there are no DTDs and no entity expansion. Namespace prefixes are ignored, which covers both the RFC 7489 schema and the DMARC 2.0 namespace.
   - The report's `policy_published/domain` must be the sending domain of the address it was sent to; anything else is dropped.

5. **Storing.** The raw attachment goes to R2 `REPORTS` at `dmarc/<sendingDomainId>/<begin day>/<sha256>.<zip|xml.gz|xml>`. The report and its records are written in one D1 batch, unique per `(sending domain, org_name, report_id)`, so a report sent twice is stored once. Identical records are merged; at most 2,000 rows are kept per report (the largest), while the totals count every record. At most 100 reports, besides the big reporters' own share below, are stored per sending domain in any 24 hours (`DMARC_DAILY_REPORT_LIMIT`), checked before the message is parsed; more are dropped as `daily report limit reached`. The report address is public, so this bounds what anyone can make Swarm store; real reporters send about one report a day each. So that one sender can't use up the whole day and crowd out the real reporters, the limit is also held per reporter (`dmarc_reports.reporter_domain`): at most 10 a day from each (`DMARC_DAILY_REPORTS_PER_REPORTER`, dropped as `daily report limit for this reporter reached`). The reporter is the organisational domain that Email Routing authenticated, read from the `Authentication-Results` header it prepends (only the first one, and only with authserv-id `mx.cloudflare.net`; any other may come from the sender): a DKIM signature that passed (`header.d`), or else the SPF identity that passed (`smtp.mailfrom`, or `smtp.helo` for an empty sender). Email Routing echoes the envelope sender, which the sender chooses, inside that header, so it is read strictly: nothing in it is trusted when the envelope sender has a parenthesis, quote, `;`, `\`, comma, white space or control character, when the header is not in the order Email Routing writes it (its DKIM results first, then DMARC and SPF once each), when a result has a stray word or a value that is not a plain address, domain or token, or when a DKIM pass has a `header.d` that is not a bare domain (or a `header.i` outside it). The envelope sender alone is never used, so a forger who types `noreply-dmarc-support@google.com` can't spend Google's share without passing SPF or DKIM for google.com. A report with nothing authenticated counts against one shared `unauthenticated` reporter, at most 5 a day (`DMARC_DAILY_UNAUTHENTICATED_REPORTS`, dropped as `daily report limit for unauthenticated reports reached`); Email Routing rejects mail that fails both SPF and DKIM before the worker runs, so real reports land here only if the header is missing. The organisational domain is the last two labels, or three under a common two-label public suffix such as `co.uk`, `co.jp` or `com.au` (a short built-in list, not the whole Public Suffix List). A shared sending service's domain (`amazonses.com`, `sendgrid.net`, `mailgun.org`, `mcsv.net`, `sparkpostmail.com`) is used only when nothing else passed, and then as the full host, so a reporter that signs with its own key besides SES's is keyed on its own domain and the service's customers don't all share one limit. A reporter that isn't established, meaning it has reports for the domain on fewer than 3 distinct days in the 30 days before today (`DMARC_ESTABLISHED_REPORTER_DAYS`), and the `unauthenticated` reporter always, stops once the domain has 50 reports today (`DMARC_DAILY_NEW_REPORTER_LIMIT`, dropped as `daily report limit for new reporters reached`). That keeps the other half of the day for reporters with a history; a sender can authenticate domains of its own, but has to keep them reporting for days before they can reach that half. Past the daily limit, an established reporter's first report of the day may still use a small reserve of 20 more (`DMARC_DAILY_RETURNING_REPORTER_RESERVE`), so domains a sender kept warm, which have all reported today by the time they fill the limit, can't also take a smaller real reporter's daily report. The big reporters (`MAJOR_DMARC_REPORTERS`: a DKIM or SPF pass as exactly `google.com` or `microsoft.com`, not a subdomain) are kept outside the 100: their reports don't count towards it and aren't dropped by it, and each is held only to its own 10 a day. The reporter is a domain Email Routing authenticated, which a forger can't pass for, so no amount of other mail, from fresh or long-warmed domains, can keep Google's or Microsoft's report out. Only domains no consumer can authenticate as are listed: anyone with a free Yahoo, Outlook.com, Mail.ru, Yandex, Zoho, Fastmail or Comcast account passes DKIM as that provider's domain, so those providers' reports take the ordinary limits like any other reporter's (Gmail mail is signed `gmail.com` and Outlook.com mail `outlook.com`, so neither passes for `google.com` or `microsoft.com`). The report's own `<email>` isn't required to match the sender: real reporters differ (Yahoo sends from `dmarc.yahoo.com` with a `yahooinc.com` address), and a forger controls both anyway.

6. **Bad input is dropped, not rejected.** A rejection would bounce to the reporter, which retries or gives up on the address. Unknown domains, malformed mail, oversized messages, mismatched domains and reports past the daily limit are logged (`dropped DMARC report`, with the reason) and accepted. Only storage failures (D1, R2) throw, so Email Routing tries again later.

## What each number means

All ranges are whole UTC days, the last 30 by default, at most 366.

| Number                  | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Aligned (DMARC)         | Messages reported as passing DMARC (SPF or DKIM aligned with the From domain), out of every message reported for the domain, for reports whose period starts in the range. A record's alignment is the reporter's `policy_evaluated` result; without one, a passing `auth_results` entry whose domain aligns (relaxed or strict, as published) counts, and SPF results scoped to HELO never do. |
| Failed DMARC            | Messages where neither SPF nor DKIM aligned.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Sources with problems   | Source IPs where SPF or DKIM did not align, those failing DMARC first, then by volume, with the disposition the reporter applied and who reported them. Organisation names come from the reports; nothing is looked up.                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Last report             | When the newest report for the domain was received.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Mailbox providers       | Deliveries sent in the range (not failed sends), by recipient domain: Gmail (`gmail.com`, `googlemail.com`), Outlook (`outlook`, `hotmail`, `live` and their country domains, `msn.com`), Yahoo and AOL (`yahoo`, `aol` and country domains, `ymail.com`, `rocketmail.com`), Apple (`icloud.com`, `me.com`, `mac.com`), other.                                                                  |
| Delivered               | Accepted by the mailbox provider (a delivery event, or a later complaint).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Bounce rate             | Bounced ÷ sent. Bounced counts hard bounces and blocks (the receiving server refused because of the sender), not soft bounces; `blocked` per provider is the blocks alone.                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Opened                  | Human opens only; machine opens (privacy proxies, scanners) are left out. Open rate is opened ÷ delivered.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Complaint rate          | Complaints ÷ delivered, overall and per provider. **Good** below 0.1% (`GMAIL_COMPLAINT_TARGET`), **watch** from 0.1%, **over** at 0.3% (`GMAIL_COMPLAINT_THRESHOLD`), Gmail's ceiling for bulk senders.                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Reputation Ramp history | Each ramp's daily cap and sends per UTC day, with that day's bounce and complaint rates. Today's bar is the live counter; its rates are only known after the day rolls over. Reading history never creates a ramp.                                                                                                                                                                              |

Reads use existing indexes: `dmarc_reports_by_domain (sending_domain_id, begin_at)` and `dmarc_records_by_report` for DMARC, `deliveries_recent_by_channel (channel_id, sent_at)` for providers, and the primary key of `reputation_ramp_days`.

## Dedicated sending

- **Configuration set (paid plans).** A paid Channel's Broadcasts go through its own SES configuration set, `swarm-<channel id>`, with reputation metrics on and an event destination for Bounce, Complaint, Delivery and Reject to the SES events topic (`SES_EVENT_TOPIC_ARN`), so the events worker sees its events as before. It is created on the Channel's first send on a paid plan, when an admin asks, or when a dedicated IP is requested. If SES refuses, the send uses the shared configuration set and tries again ten minutes later; a send is never blocked by it. Free Channels share the configuration set in `SES_CONFIGURATION_SET`.
- **Dedicated IP (Pro and up).** An admin requests it; Swarm creates a managed dedicated IP pool (`CreateDedicatedIpPool`, `ScalingMode: MANAGED`), `swarm-ip-<channel id>`, and attaches it to the Channel's configuration set (`PutConfigurationSetDeliveryOptions`). Status moves `requested` → `warming` → `active`. A request the provider refused stays `requested` and the send worker's minute cron retries it; the cron also reads warm-up (`GetDedicatedIps`) at most hourly, and "Check now" reads it immediately. `active` means SES reports every IP in the pool as warmed up. Managed pools are billed by AWS; costs are passed through at AWS's price with no markup, and the dashboard links to Amazon SES pricing rather than quoting a price.
- **Plans.** On the free plan the configuration-set and dedicated-IP requests answer `403 plan_required`; dedicated IPs follow `PLAN_LIMITS[plan].dedicatedIps`.
- **Development.** With `MAIL_ADAPTER=dev` (development and test only) a fake records each call (`devDedicatedCalls`) and reports every pool as warmed up.

## Suppressions

A suppression is an address a Channel never mails. Table `suppressions` (`0003`, Channel scope in `0026_suppressions.sql`, bounce columns in `0067_bounce_policy.sql`); code in `@swarm/engine` `suppressions.ts`, `provider-suppressions.ts`, `feedback.ts` and `bounces.ts`.

- **Scope.** Bounces (when the workspace's bounce rules say so, see "Bounces" below), complaints and Privacy Center erasures are workspace-wide within the workspace whose mail caused them. They never change another workspace's subscribers: on a shared install, each workspace is its own publisher with its own relationship to the address (see "One SES account, many workspaces" below). An admin can add a manual suppression for one Channel (the default) or, with a workspace admin seat, for the whole workspace. Every send-time check (snapshot, Flows, Direct Send, confirmations, imports, Capture Forms) reads "workspace-wide, or for this Channel". The send consumer applies the same check to every recipient again just before handing them to the provider, so someone who leaves after the snapshot or after a Flow queued their email gets nothing, including on a retry; the row is marked `skipped`.
- **Unsubscribes are not suppressions.** Unsubscribing is the Member's status in that Channel (Members are per Channel), so it never stops the workspace's other Channels. A complaint is different: it suppresses the address workspace-wide and marks every Channel's Member `complained`.
- **Removing.** An admin can remove manual and bounce suppressions (a workspace-wide one needs a workspace admin). Complaints and erased addresses stay (409). Removing a bounce suppression is how an admin says the address works again: the workspace's Members of it get back the status saved in `members.bounced_from` when the bounce stopped them (a paused one comes back `active`, since its pause date was cleared). A Member bounced before `0067` has no saved status and comes back `unsubscribed` if it had left, `active` if it had agreed (`consent_at` or `confirmed_at`), otherwise `pending`, so nobody is mailed without agreeing. The address's bounce history and counters are deleted, so the rules count from zero. This is the only way out of `bounced`; the domain's status rules (`@swarm/domain` `audience.ts`) still let no one else move a Member out of it. Audit `suppression.removed` with `restored`, and the Circles of those Channels are refreshed.
- **SES account-level list, out.** A hard-bounce suppression queues a row in `provider_suppression_sync` (a hard bounce the rules did not stop yet, or held for an admin, does not), and removing one queues a removal, as does "Keep mailing" on a held hard bounce. The jobs worker's minute cron applies due rows with `PutSuppressedDestination` (reason `BOUNCE`) and `DeleteSuppressedDestination`. Both calls are idempotent, a row is leased before the call, and provider errors retry with backoff from 30 seconds up to an hour, eight attempts in all; a rejection or the last failure leaves the row `failed` and is logged. A removal is applied whatever other workspaces hold. Complaints, soft-bounce and manual suppressions never go to SES; complaint rows queued by an older version are dropped without a call. Bounces that SES itself reported as `OnAccountSuppressionList` aren't sent back.
- **SES account-level list, back.** Nothing is read back. An address on the SES list is learned when a workspace sends to it: SES refuses the send and reports a permanent bounce with subtype `OnAccountSuppressionList`, which suppresses the address in the sending workspace only, as a hard bounce, and marks that workspace's subscribers `bounced`. A send to a listed address therefore never goes out, from any workspace.
- **One SES account, many workspaces.** The SES list belongs to the AWS account, so every workspace on an install shares it. That is why only hard bounces go on it: a mailbox that does not exist does not exist for anyone, and SES would refuse the send anyway. A complaint is different. It says the person did not want one workspace's mail; they may still want another publisher's, and suppressing them there would let one tenant's subscriber change another tenant's list and tell the second tenant something about the first. So a complaint suppresses and unsubscribes in the workspace that sent the mail and nowhere else. The cost is the account's complaint rate: another workspace mailing that person again could draw a second complaint, but that workspace has its own consent from them and its own complaint checks (Warm-up pauses, per-workspace suppression). Removals follow the same rule. Removing a hard bounce removes the address from the SES list even if another workspace also has it suppressed; that workspace's own suppression keeps stopping its sends, and skipping the removal would have told the first workspace that someone else holds the address. On a single-owner install this changes little: the owner's own events suppress the owner's own workspace, as before, and a listed address is caught at the first send instead of up to five minutes before it.
- **The SES account's own setting.** SES can also add addresses to the list by itself, account-wide, for the reasons set in the account's suppression attributes, and new accounts have both `BOUNCE` and `COMPLAINT` on. The installer (terminal and browser setup) sets it to `BOUNCE` only with `PutAccountSuppressionAttributes` during SES setup, so complaints stay with the workspace that caused them; repeating it is harmless, and `pnpm swarm doctor` reports an account that is not set that way. On an install made before that, run `pnpm swarm` once with the AWS admin key, or `aws sesv2 put-account-suppression-attributes --suppressed-reasons BOUNCE`. Complaint entries added before this change stay on the list until removed; list them with `aws sesv2 list-suppressed-destinations --reasons COMPLAINT` and remove each with `aws sesv2 delete-suppressed-destination --email-address <address>`. Swarm's own complaint suppressions are untouched by this, so the workspaces that received the complaints keep them.
- **The other services.** With `MAIL_ADAPTER=resend`, `mailgun`, `postmark` or `smtp`, `suppressionList()` is null. Nothing goes to AWS: the minute cron discards queued `provider_suppression_sync` rows of any status. Resend's own list is not synced, so a suppression removed in Swarm stays on it until someone removes it in Resend; a send before then comes back as `email.suppressed`, a hard bounce, and suppresses the address again (docs/self-hosting.md).
- **Development.** With `MAIL_ADAPTER=dev` an in-memory fake records each call (`devSuppressionCalls`) and holds the list (`devSuppressedDestinations`).

## Bounces

How a workspace reacts when its email turns out undeliverable. Tables `bounce_policies`, `bounce_events` and `bounce_states`, plus `suppressions.bounce_rule`, `bounce_cause`, `bounce_counted`, `bounce_days` and `members.bounced_from` (`0067_bounce_policy.sql`). Code: `@swarm/engine` `bounce-classifier.ts` (pure) and `bounces.ts`; shapes, limits and defaults: `@swarm/domain` `bounces.ts`. Writers' explanation: [deliverability guide](deliverability-guide.md#bounces) and [audience](audience.md#bounces).

- **Mapping.** Each provider's event is first mapped onto SES's shape (`feedback.ts`, `smtp-feedback.ts`), keeping the recipient's status code in `bouncedRecipients[].status`: SES's enhanced code, Mailgun's `enhanced-code` or its SMTP reply code (never its own 6xx codes), a delivery status report's `Status`. Postmark's `Type`, Mailgun's `reason` and SES/Resend's `bounceSubType` travel as `bounceSubType`.
- **Classification.** `classifyBounce` gives a kind and a cause, from the strongest signal down:
  1. Types that are not bounces (Postmark `AutoResponder`, `ChallengeVerification`, `Subscribe`, `Unsubscribe`, `AddressChange`, `OpenRelayTest`, `SMTPApiError`, `InboundError`, `TemplateRenderingFailed`) are ignored: nothing is stored.
  2. The provider's own list (SES `OnAccountSuppressionList` and `Suppressed`, Resend `Suppressed`, Mailgun `suppress-bounce`, `suppress-unsubscribe`, `suppress-complaint`, Postmark `ManuallyDeactivated`): hard, `provider_list`.
  3. Provider block types (Postmark `Blocked`, `DMARCPolicy`, `SpamNotification`, `VirusNotification`; SES and Resend `ContentRejected`, `AttachmentRejected`; Mailgun `espblock`): blocked.
  4. The receiving server's words, blocks first: blocklist names (Spamhaus, Barracuda, SpamCop, Microsoft S3150 ...), authentication (unauthenticated, SPF, DKIM, DMARC, PTR, 5.7.23 to 5.7.27, 5.7.509), content, reputation (unsolicited, spam, banned), rate limits (Yahoo TSS codes, "too many"); then mailbox full, too large, greylisting, no such domain, no such user, a disabled or switched-off mailbox, relaying denied.
  5. The enhanced status code (RFC 3463): X.7.x blocked, X.6.x blocked (content), X.2.2 full, X.2.1 unavailable, X.2.3 and X.3.4 too large, 5.1.0/5.1.1/5.1.3/5.1.6/5.1.10 no mailbox, 5.1.2 and 5.4.4 no domain.
  6. The basic reply code (RFC 5321): 552 storage, 521 and 556 no mail at all.
  7. The provider's own call: SES `NoEmail`, Postmark `HardBounce` and `BadEmailAddress`, any `Permanent` bounce: hard. `Transient`, `MailboxFull`, `MessageTooLarge`, Postmark `DnsError`, Mailgun `old`: soft. `Undetermined`: soft.

  Hard needs a permanent signal (a `Permanent` type or a 5.x.x code) and nothing that says temporary (a 4.x.x code, a `Transient` type, Mailgun `old`): when unsure it is soft. Causes: hard `no_mailbox`, `no_domain`, `provider_list`, `rejected`; soft `mailbox_full`, `mailbox_unavailable`, `too_large`, `greylisted`, `temporary`, `unknown`; blocked `reputation`, `blocklist`, `content`, `authentication`, `rate_limited`, `policy`. The samples the classifier is tested against are in `packages/engine/tests/bounce-classifier.test.ts`.

- **History and counters.** A bounce is stored once per email and kind in `bounce_events` (unique `(workspace_id, email, send_id, kind)`), so Mailgun's report on every retry and SNS delivering twice count once. An address that is still mailed has one `bounce_states` row: `soft_streak` counts emails in a row that bounced softly. A Delivery event sets it back to 0, and at the next soft bounce an email to the address's Members sent between the last counted one and this one that was delivered, opened or clicked restarts it at 1, which covers mail servers that report no deliveries and events that arrive out of order. Blocks neither add to the streak nor reset it, and a block never replaces an address's last bounce (`last_kind`, `last_cause`, `last_code`, `last_detail`, `last_bounced_at`) unless that was a block too: a block is about the sender, so a hold keeps naming the bounces that made it, and a later stop records their cause. `0069` put back the newest hard or soft bounce on rows a block had overwritten. Both tables keep 90 days (`BOUNCE_LIMITS.historyDays`, `retention.ts`), are erased with the address (`privacy.ts`) and are part of a data export (`bounces`). `soft_bounces` is no longer written; `0067` copied its rows into `bounce_events`.
- **The policy.** `bounce_policies` holds one row per workspace that saved its rules. Without one the workspace has `DEFAULT_BOUNCE_POLICY`: stop at the first hard bounce, stop after 3 emails in a row bounced softly, no time-window rule, ask before stopping a paid plan. The choices: hard bounces 1, or 2 within 30 days; the soft streak 2 to 10, or off; "N soft bounces within D days" with N 2 to 10 and D 7, 14, 30, 60 or 90, or off. Both soft rules off means soft bounces never stop mailing. Complaints are not part of the policy: the first one suppresses and unsubscribes, as before.
- **Stopping.** A matching rule runs `stopStatements`: a workspace-wide suppression, `hard_bounce` for the rules `hard` and `hard_repeat` and `soft_bounce` for `soft_streak` and `soft_window`, with the rule, the cause, what it counted and the server's words in `detail` (a block's cause is never recorded there: `bounce_cause` is then null); the workspace's Members of the address to `bounced`, their status saved in `bounced_from`; the counters row deleted; for a hard bounce the SES account-level list. Every hard bounce and block marks its delivery `bounced` with `bounce_type` `<kind>:<cause>`, so it counts in send reports, in `providerBreakdown` (`blocked` per mailbox provider), in the check-up's bounce rate and `provider_blocks` finding, and in the warm-up pause (`checkRampHealth`), whether or not a rule stops the address. A soft bounce leaves the delivery as it was.
- **Paid plans.** With `ask_for_paid`, an address whose Members in the workspace include one with a current paid plan (`member_entitlements` kind `paid`, started and not expired, any source: a payment, a gift or a free grant, as the inactive-reader cleanup reads it) is held instead of stopped: `held_at`, `held_rule`, `held_counted`, `held_days`, and `hold_notice = 'due'`. Nothing is suppressed and the address keeps being mailed. The jobs worker's minute cron (`sendBounceHoldNotices`) emails the workspace's owners and admins (seats) once per hold from `notifications@<PLATFORM_MAIL_DOMAIN>`, claiming it first as ramp pause notices do; audit `bounce.hold_notified`. A delivered email lets go of a hold a soft rule made. An admin decides: `stop` suppresses as the rule would have; `keep` clears the hold, sets `counts_from` so earlier bounces no longer count, and for a hard rule queues the address's removal from the SES list. Audit `bounce.hold_decided`.
- **Changing the policy.** New rules apply from each address's next bounce, with its stored counts. `previewBouncePolicy` evaluates them against the counters and history of addresses that are still mailed (a Member pending, active or paused, and no workspace-wide suppression) and counts those it would stop and hold. With `applyToPast` the save stops or holds them at once, at most 500 per save (`remaining` says how many are left). An address already held that the rules would hold again is not counted, in the preview or against the 500, so a workspace with many holds still reaches everyone else; a held address the rules would now stop (the plan ended, or "ask me first" is off) is counted and stopped. Audit `bounce_policy.changed` with both policies and what was applied.
- **The providers' own lists.** SES (whose account suppression attributes the installer sets to `BOUNCE`), Resend, Mailgun and Postmark stop sending to an address after its first hard bounce on their own. Under "2 hard bounces within 30 days" the second send is therefore normally refused by the provider and reported as `provider_list`, which counts as the second hard bounce. The option mostly helps a person's own mail server, and the dashboard says so.

## Reputation Ramp pauses

A ramp pauses on its own when the bounce or complaint rate goes over its threshold, either right after an SES event or at the start of a UTC day. The pause marks the ramp `pause_notice = 'due'`. The jobs worker's minute cron then emails the Channel's owners and admins (workspace seats, less anyone a Channel role lowers) once from `notifications@<PLATFORM_MAIL_DOMAIN>`. The email names the domain, gives the reason with the rate and the threshold, and links to `<APP_ORIGIN>/channels/<id>/deliverability`. The ramp is claimed (`'sent'`) before mailing, so a pause is announced at most once; if every send failed, the claim is undone and the next run tries again. Resuming clears the mark; pausing a ramp that is already paused changes nothing, so the notice still goes out (and the pause stays the ramp's own, `paused_by = 'system'`, see [warm-up.md](warm-up.md)). Audit: `ramp.paused` and `ramp.pause_notified`.

| `PUT /api/v1/workspaces/{workspaceId}/bounce-policy`      | admin  | Change them, optionally applying them to past bounces         |
| `POST …/bounce-policy/preview`                            | admin  | How many addresses the rules would stop or hold now           |
| `GET /api/v1/channels/{channelId}/bounce-holds`           | viewer | Subscribers on a paid plan waiting for a decision             |
| `POST …/bounce-holds/{holdId}`                            | admin  | Stop or keep mailing a held address (workspace admin)         |
| `GET /api/v1/members/{memberId}/bounces`                  | viewer | One subscriber's bounces, counts, hold and suppression        |

`from` and `to` (`YYYY-MM-DD`) set the range on the two reads that take one.

## Check-up

`GET …/deliverability/checkup` (viewer) runs every check for a Channel and answers `{ checkedAt, verdict, score, counts, from, findings[] }`. A finding is `{ id, subject, severity, title, meaning, fix[], link, external[] }`: `id` is one of `CHECKUP_FINDING_IDS` and never changes, `subject` tells apart findings of a check that runs per domain or per blocklist, `severity` is `problem`, `warning`, `unknown` (could not be checked) or `good`, and `link.to` names a dashboard page (`CHECKUP_LINK_TARGETS`). Findings are ordered by severity, then by what hurts delivery most (`IMPACT_ORDER` in `packages/engine/src/checkup.ts`). `verdict` is `problems`, `warnings` or `healthy`. `score` is 100 minus 20 for each problem and 5 for each warning, never below 0; unknown findings change nothing. Every limit is in `CHECKUP` (`packages/domain/src/p7-deliverability.ts`).

- **Two halves.** `gatherCheckupFacts` reads the default sender, the sending domains a sender uses (at most three), their DNS records and DMARC record, DMARC report totals, 30 days of `providerBreakdown`, warm-up, list settings and blocklists. Each read is settled on its own. `buildCheckup` is pure and turns the facts into findings. A read or a group of checks that fails becomes an `unknown` finding; the route never answers 5xx because one check failed.
- **Copy.** Finding text is written on the server in the dashboard's plain words and shown as it is.
- **Blocklists** (`packages/mail/src/blocklist.ts`). The sending domain and its registered parent are looked up on Spamhaus DBL, SURBL and URIBL over the same DNS-over-HTTPS lookups as the record checks, with a 3 second timeout per list. These lists refuse queries from public resolvers, so an answer counts as `listed` only when it is one of the list's documented listing codes, and as `clean` only when the list's permanent test entry answered as listed through the same resolver. Refusal codes (Spamhaus `127.255.255.x`, bit 1 on SURBL and URIBL), silence without that proof, timeouts and errors are all `unknown`, reported with a link to the list's own lookup page. Results are cached in KV `CACHE` for 15 minutes (`checkup:blocklists:v1:<domain>`). Blocklists are asked only by this route, never while sending.
- **Development.** With `DNS_RESOLVER=dev` there is no network: blocklist answers are read from KV `CACHE` keys `dev:dns:A:<name>` (a JSON list of addresses), so with nothing seeded every list is `unknown`.

## Content advice in the pre-send checklist

`GET /api/v1/dispatches/{dispatchId}/preflight` also answers `advice: { id, message }[]`: non-blocking advice computed from the rendered email by `checkContent` (`packages/mail/src/content-check.ts`). It never changes `ready`. Ids are `CONTENT_ADVICE_IDS`, limits `CONTENT_LIMITS`, and the two lists it matches against are `SPAM_TRIGGER_PHRASES` and `PUBLIC_LINK_SHORTENERS` in the same file. Links to hosts the workspace owns (its Shorthop address and short domain, the Channel's branded link domains, the tracking origin) are never called a public shortener or a mismatch. Image sizes come from the media library. The dashboard lists advice under "Inbox advice" in the send panel, apart from the checks.
